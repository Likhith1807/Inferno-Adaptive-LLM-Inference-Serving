02 · Inside vLLM

End to end: at parity with vLLM's default kernel at one sequence, behind with more

Two claims I had to retract

“Beats vLLM's Marlin kernel.” It didn't. My Marlin timing was about 2× too slow and I only caught it because end-to-end tokens/s disagreed with the kernel times. Re-measured in both engine modes, Marlin runs at the bandwidth limit on the big layers.

“98% of peak bandwidth.” The benchmark let the GPU's L2 cache serve ~6% of the traffic. After a rotation-size sweep the numbers were regenerated and are lower.

Both errors are in the audit trail of the technical report.

08 · Limits

What this does not show

One small GPU

Replicas shared a single 8 GB laptop GPU. The adaptive idea is untested where each replica has its own device (cloud configs are written, not run).

Parity, not a win

Inside vLLM the kernel matches the default Marlin path at one sequence and is slower from four. Marlin is at the bandwidth limit on the large layers, so there is no headroom to beat it at batch 1 on this GPU.

Causes are hypotheses

Why v5/v6 fixed the K=1536 slowdown (staging phase or lane imbalance) is not confirmed: GPU performance counters are blocked by a Windows driver setting, so Nsight cannot run.

Small samples

4 to 8 repetitions per cell: enough for large effects, not for differences near 1%.
